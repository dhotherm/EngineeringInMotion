Engineering in Motion

A grassroots movement to transform how we build software—from traditional IT delivery to modern product development.

Five core practices. One shared mission. Built by engineers, for engineers.

Why Engineering in Motion?

The world is moving fast. To compete with modern tech companies and fintechs, we need to transform how we work—not just what we build.

Speed Matters

Our business strategy requires us to deliver 4x faster than we do today. That's not incremental improvement—it's fundamental transformation.

Culture Drives Outcomes

The best technology in the world won't help if our culture is slow, siloed, and risk-averse. We need to rewire how we collaborate and make decisions.

Talent Follows Excellence

Top engineers want to work where engineering excellence is valued, where they can grow, and where they can see the impact of their work.

Five Core Practices

These aren't mandates from leadership. They're the proven patterns that high-performing engineering organizations use to move fast and build great products.

📊

DORA Metrics at Team Level

Make performance visible. Every team tracks deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, and time to restore—and uses these metrics to continuously improve.

What this looks like:

  • Teams own their metrics, not leadership
  • Data drives retrospectives and improvements
  • Celebrate progress, not perfection
  • Share learnings across teams
🌐

Open by Default

Default to transparency. Share work in progress, document decisions in the open, and make information accessible to everyone who needs it.

What this looks like:

  • Engineering blog for sharing learnings
  • Public roadmaps and strategy docs
  • Open source internal tools when possible
  • Default to public channels, not private DMs
💬

Slack as Communication Backbone

Move from email to real-time collaboration. Use Slack (or similar) as the primary way teams communicate, make decisions, and stay aligned.

What this looks like:

  • Public channels for teams and projects
  • Threaded conversations for context
  • Integrations that bring work into chat
  • Reduce meetings, increase async communication
🎯

Embedded Capability in Teams

End the ticket-and-wait model. Embed specialized skills (security, data, platform) directly into product teams so they can move autonomously.

What this looks like:

  • Security engineers pair with product teams
  • Platform teams enable, not gatekeep
  • Reduce handoffs and dependencies
  • Build expertise across the organization
🚀

Clear Career Paths

Show engineers how they can grow. Create transparent career ladders that value both technical depth and leadership breadth.

What this looks like:

  • Dual tracks: Individual Contributor and Leadership
  • Clear expectations at each level
  • Regular career conversations, not annual reviews
  • Invest in learning and development

How We Show Up

Beyond the practices, here's how we work together as we build this movement.

Start Small, Think Big

We don't need permission to improve how we work. Start with your team, prove the value, and let it spread organically.

Ship and Learn

Perfect is the enemy of good. Get something out there, gather feedback, iterate. Velocity beats perfection every time.

Build in Public

Share what you're learning—the wins and the failures. Transparency builds trust and accelerates everyone's learning.

Question Everything

If something doesn't make sense, ask why. Challenge the status quo respectfully, but challenge it. Better ideas come from healthy debate.

Care Deeply About Craft

We're not just writing code—we're solving problems that matter. Take pride in the quality of what you build and how you build it.

Lift Others Up

Great engineers make those around them better. Share knowledge, mentor others, and create space for everyone to grow.

Getting Started

You don't need to adopt everything at once. Pick what makes sense for your team and start there.

1

Pick One Practice

Choose the practice that will have the biggest impact for your team right now. Start small and build momentum.

2

Run an Experiment

Try it for a sprint or two. Measure what changes. Gather feedback from your team about what's working and what's not.

3

Share Your Learnings

Document what you tried and what you learned. Share it with other teams so they can learn from your experience.

4

Iterate and Expand

Based on what worked, iterate on your approach. When you're ready, add another practice. Keep the momentum going.
